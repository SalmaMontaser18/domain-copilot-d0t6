# Hypertension Treatment Guideline (Cardiolan)

## 1. Purpose and Scope

1.1 This guideline sets out the synthetic standard of care for the initiation, titration, monitoring, and discontinuation of Cardiolan in adults with hypertension. It is a fictional document created for software testing and has no clinical validity.

1.2 It applies to adult patients aged 18 years and older with a confirmed diagnosis of hypertension, as defined in Section 2.

1.3 It does not apply to pregnancy, to hypertensive emergencies requiring parenteral therapy, or to patients under 18 years of age.

1.4 Cardiolan is the sole antihypertensive agent addressed in this guideline. Other fictional agents are referenced only where they interact with Cardiolan or where a comorbidity changes management.

## 2. Definitions and Diagnosis

2.1 Hypertension is confirmed when the mean of at least two seated office readings, taken on at least two separate visits, is 140/90 mmHg or higher.

2.2 Home blood pressure monitoring may be used to confirm diagnosis. A home average of 135/85 mmHg or higher over 7 days (two readings morning and evening, first day discarded) is considered confirmatory.

2.3 Severe hypertension is defined as a sustained reading of 180/110 mmHg or higher. Patients with severe hypertension and symptoms such as chest pain, confusion, or visual disturbance must be referred for urgent assessment and are outside the scope of this guideline.

2.4 The standard office measurement requires the patient to be seated and rested for 5 minutes, with the arm supported at heart level and an appropriately sized cuff.

## 3. Treatment Targets

3.1 For most adults under 65 years, the target is a clinic reading below 130/80 mmHg.

3.2 For adults aged 65 years and older, the target is a clinic reading below 140/90 mmHg, provided the treatment is well tolerated.

3.3 For patients with type 2 diabetes treated with Glucorex, the target is below 130/80 mmHg regardless of age, unless orthostatic symptoms occur.

3.4 For patients with chronic kidney disease receiving Renaprotect, the target is below 130/80 mmHg, with attention to the renal safeguards in Section 6.

3.5 Targets should be reached within 3 months of treatment initiation wherever feasible.

## 4. Dosing and Titration

4.1 The recommended starting dose of Cardiolan is 5 mg once daily, taken in the morning with or without food.

4.2 If the target is not reached after 4 weeks, the dose may be increased to 10 mg once daily.

4.3 If the target is not reached after a further 4 weeks at 10 mg, the dose may be increased to the maximum of 20 mg once daily. Doses above 20 mg per day are not recommended.

4.4 For patients aged 75 years and older, or those with frailty, the starting dose is 2.5 mg once daily, with titration steps of no more than one level every 4 weeks.

4.5 Cardiolan is not intended for use as a twice-daily regimen. A missed dose should be taken as soon as remembered unless it is within 12 hours of the next scheduled dose, in which case it should be skipped.

## 5. Contraindications and Cautions

5.1 Cardiolan is contraindicated in patients with a documented hypersensitivity to Cardiolan or any of its excipients.

5.2 Cardiolan is contraindicated in patients with a resting heart rate below 50 beats per minute, second- or third-degree atrioventricular block without a pacemaker, or cardiogenic shock.

5.3 Cardiolan is contraindicated in pregnancy. Women of childbearing potential must be counselled on reliable contraception, and Cardiolan must be stopped promptly if pregnancy is confirmed.

5.4 Caution is advised in patients with a history of symptomatic hypotension, a baseline systolic pressure below 110 mmHg, or severe hepatic impairment. In severe hepatic impairment, the maximum dose is 10 mg once daily.

5.5 Patients should be advised to rise slowly from a seated or lying position, particularly during the first 2 weeks of treatment and after each dose increase.

## 6. Renal Considerations

6.1 Renal function must be measured before initiation and again 2 weeks after starting treatment or after any dose increase.

6.2 For patients with an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) of 60 mL/min/1.73 m² or higher, no dose adjustment is required.

6.3 For an eGFR of 30 to 59 mL/min/1.73 m², the maximum dose is 10 mg once daily.

6.4 For an eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73 m², the maximum dose is 5 mg once daily, and nephrology advice should be sought before initiation.

6.5 If serum creatinine rises by more than 30% from baseline within 2 weeks of a dose change, Cardiolan should be reduced to the previous dose level and renal function rechecked in 1 week.

6.6 Serum potassium above 5.5 mmol/L requires withholding Cardiolan until the cause is identified and the level has returned below 5.0 mmol/L.

## 7. Drug Interactions

7.1 **Renaprotect.** Co-administration with Renaprotect is permitted and is common in hypertension with kidney disease. Because both agents may raise serum potassium, potassium and creatinine must be checked at 1 week and 4 weeks after the second agent is started. The combined maximum Cardiolan dose is 10 mg once daily while the patient is receiving Renaprotect at a dose of 40 mg or more per day.

7.2 **Glucorex.** Cardiolan may mask some early signs of hypoglycaemia, such as tremor and palpitations, in patients taking Glucorex. Patients should be counselled that sweating remains a reliable warning sign, and home glucose monitoring should be continued.

7.3 **Pulmavent.** Cardiolan may partially antagonise the effect of Pulmavent. In patients with asthma or chronic obstructive disease, Cardiolan should be used with caution, and Pulmavent response should be reviewed 2 weeks after Cardiolan is started. If wheeze worsens, Cardiolan should be stopped and an alternative class considered.

7.4 **Neurazol.** Neurazol may modestly lower blood pressure. When Neurazol is added to a stable Cardiolan regimen, blood pressure should be rechecked within 2 weeks, and the Cardiolan dose should be reduced by one step if systolic pressure falls below 110 mmHg.

7.5 **Thyrosan.** Changes in Thyrosan dosing alter heart rate and may change the effect of Cardiolan. Heart rate and blood pressure should be reviewed 4 weeks after any Thyrosan dose change of 25 micrograms or more.

7.6 **Antibiox.** A course of Antibiox of up to 14 days does not require adjustment of Cardiolan. During acute illness with vomiting, diarrhoea, or poor oral intake, Cardiolan should be withheld temporarily and restarted 24 to 48 hours after normal hydration is restored.

7.7 **Zentavir.** No clinically significant interaction between Zentavir and Cardiolan is recognised. Routine monitoring applies.

## 8. Monitoring and Follow-Up

8.1 During titration, blood pressure and heart rate should be reviewed every 4 weeks until the target is reached.

8.2 Once the target is stable, review is required every 6 months, with serum creatinine, eGFR, and potassium measured at least once every 12 months.

8.3 Home monitoring is encouraged. Patients should record readings twice daily for 7 days before each scheduled review.

8.4 Adherence should be assessed at every visit. If adherence is below 80% of prescribed doses, causes should be explored before dose escalation.

8.5 A resting heart rate below 50 beats per minute at any review requires dose reduction by one step and an electrocardiogram.

## 9. Adverse Effects and Discontinuation

9.1 Common adverse effects (affecting 1 in 100 to 1 in 10 patients) include dizziness, fatigue, headache, and peripheral oedema. These usually resolve within 2 weeks and rarely require withdrawal.

9.2 Uncommon adverse effects include persistent dry cough, symptomatic bradycardia, and elevated liver enzymes.

9.3 Cardiolan should be discontinued immediately in cases of angioedema, jaundice, or syncope attributable to the drug.

9.4 For planned discontinuation, the dose should be reduced by one step every 2 weeks, with blood pressure monitored weekly, to avoid rebound hypertension.

9.5 Patients should not stop Cardiolan abruptly unless a contraindicating event, as described in 9.3, has occurred.

## 10. Special Populations

10.1 **Older adults.** Use the reduced starting dose in 4.4 and screen for orthostatic hypotension at every review by measuring standing blood pressure after 1 and 3 minutes.

10.2 **Patients with diabetes.** Apply the target in 3.3 and the interaction guidance in 7.2.

10.3 **Patients with kidney disease.** Apply the dose caps in Section 6 and the combination rule in 7.1.

10.4 **Patients with obstructive airway disease.** Apply 7.3 and consider alternative classes if bronchospasm occurs.

10.5 **Women of childbearing potential.** Apply 5.3 and document contraceptive counselling at initiation and annually.

## 11. Patient Counselling

11.1 Patients should be told that Cardiolan is a long-term therapy and that blood pressure often causes no symptoms.

11.2 Patients should be advised to report swelling of the lips or face, fainting, a very slow pulse, or yellowing of the skin without delay.

11.3 Lifestyle measures, including limiting dietary sodium to under 2 g per day, regular physical activity of at least 150 minutes per week, and weight management, should accompany drug therapy.

## 12. Illustrative Case Note

12.1 Patient A, aged 78, with type 2 diabetes treated with Glucorex and an eGFR of 52 mL/min/1.73 m², presents with a mean clinic reading of 156/92 mmHg. Creatinine and potassium are checked at 2 weeks under 6.1, and the patient is counselled on hypoglycaemia awareness under 7.2.

12.2 After 8 weeks, the dose has reached 5 mg once daily and the reading is 128/78 mmHg, which meets the target in 3.3. Review continues every 6 months under 8.2.
