# Community Pneumonia Guideline (Antibiox)

## 1. Purpose and Scope

1.1 This guideline sets out the former synthetic standard of care for the assessment and treatment of community-acquired pneumonia in adults using Antibiox. It is a fictional document created for software testing and has no clinical validity.

1.2 It applies to adults aged 18 years and older with suspected or confirmed pneumonia acquired outside hospital, or within 48 hours of hospital admission.

1.3 It does not apply to hospital-acquired pneumonia, aspiration pneumonia, patients with severe immunosuppression, or patients under 18 years of age.

1.4 Antibiox is the sole antibiotic addressed in this guideline. Other fictional agents are referenced only where they interact with Antibiox or where a comorbidity changes management.

## 2. Diagnosis

2.1 Pneumonia is suspected in a patient with acute cough and at least one of the following: fever of 38.5 °C or higher, new focal chest signs, breathlessness, or pleuritic chest pain.

2.2 Diagnosis is confirmed by a new infiltrate on chest radiograph. Where radiography is not available within 24 hours, treatment may begin on clinical grounds alone.

2.3 Oxygen saturation should be measured in every patient. A saturation below 94% on room air indicates at least moderate severity.

2.4 Blood tests for C-reactive protein, urea, and creatinine should be taken in all patients, regardless of severity.

## 3. Severity Assessment

3.1 Severity is assessed using the synthetic CRB score, which awards 1 point for each of the following: new confusion, respiratory rate of 30 breaths per minute or higher, and systolic blood pressure below 90 mmHg or diastolic blood pressure of 60 mmHg or lower.

3.2 A CRB score of 0 indicates mild pneumonia and suitability for home treatment.

3.3 A CRB score of 1 indicates moderate pneumonia. Same-day hospital assessment is recommended, and home treatment is acceptable only with review within 48 hours.

3.4 A CRB score of 2 or higher indicates severe pneumonia, and the patient must be admitted urgently.

3.5 Clinical judgement overrides the score. Patients with an oxygen saturation below 88% on room air must be admitted regardless of the score.

## 4. Dosing and Duration

4.1 For mild pneumonia, the recommended dose of Antibiox is 250 mg four times daily, taken orally every 6 hours, for 7 days.

4.2 For moderate pneumonia treated at home, the dose is 500 mg four times daily, taken orally every 6 hours, for 10 days.

4.3 For severe pneumonia, Antibiox is given intravenously at 500 mg every 6 hours for the first 72 hours, then switched to oral 500 mg four times daily once the patient is clinically stable, for a total of 14 days.

4.4 The maximum dose of Antibiox is 2000 mg per day. Doses above this limit are not recommended.

4.5 Antibiox should be taken on an empty stomach, at least 1 hour before or 2 hours after a meal. Treatment should be started within 8 hours of diagnosis in all patients and within 4 hours in patients with severe pneumonia.

4.6 A missed dose should be taken as soon as remembered unless it is within 2 hours of the next scheduled dose, in which case it should be skipped.

## 5. Contraindications and Cautions

5.1 Antibiox is contraindicated in patients with a documented hypersensitivity to Antibiox or any of its excipients.

5.2 Antibiox is contraindicated in patients with a previous episode of severe cutaneous reaction or anaphylaxis attributed to Antibiox.

5.3 Caution is advised in patients with a history of antibiotic-associated colitis, severe hepatic impairment, or a history of seizures.

5.4 Antibiox is not recommended in pregnancy unless no alternative is available, and the decision must be documented.

5.5 Patients should be advised to report persistent diarrhoea, rash, or yellowing of the skin without delay.

## 6. Renal Considerations

6.1 Serum creatinine and eGFR must be measured before initiation in all patients.

6.2 For an eGFR of 50 mL/min/1.73 m² or higher, no dose adjustment is required.

6.3 For an eGFR of 30 to 49 mL/min/1.73 m², the dose is reduced by half while the interval of every 6 hours is unchanged, and the maximum dose is 1000 mg per day.

6.4 For an eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73 m², the dose is 250 mg every 12 hours, and renal function should be reviewed after 72 hours.

6.5 For patients undergoing dialysis, the dose is 250 mg once daily, given after the dialysis session on dialysis days.

6.6 Renal function should be rechecked at 72 hours in any patient with an eGFR below 50 mL/min/1.73 m² at baseline.

## 7. Drug Interactions

7.1 **Cardiolan.** A course of Antibiox of up to 14 days does not require adjustment of Cardiolan. During acute illness with vomiting, diarrhoea, or poor oral intake, Cardiolan should be withheld temporarily and restarted 72 hours after normal hydration is restored.

7.2 **Glucorex.** A course of Antibiox of up to 14 days does not require Glucorex adjustment. During acute illness with vomiting, diarrhoea, or poor oral intake, Glucorex should be withheld temporarily and restarted 72 hours after normal eating and drinking are re-established. Capillary glucose should be checked at least twice daily during the illness.

7.3 **Pulmavent.** Pulmavent may be used alongside Antibiox for symptom relief. Patients using Pulmavent more than 6 times per day despite treatment should be reassessed for severity within 48 hours.

7.4 **Renaprotect.** In patients taking Renaprotect, creatinine and potassium must be checked at 72 hours after starting Antibiox, because dehydration during pneumonia may reduce renal function.

7.5 **Thyrosan.** No dose adjustment of Thyrosan is required during a course of Antibiox. Thyroid function should not be tested for 4 weeks after an acute severe illness unless clinically indicated.

7.6 **Neurazol.** No clinically significant interaction between Neurazol and Antibiox is recognised.

7.7 **Zentavir.** No clinically significant interaction between Zentavir and Antibiox is recognised. If influenza-like illness is suspected alongside pneumonia, antiviral treatment decisions are outside the scope of this guideline.

## 8. Monitoring and Follow-Up

8.1 Patients treated at home with a CRB score of 0 should be reviewed by telephone or in person at 72 hours.

8.2 Patients treated at home with a CRB score of 1 must be reviewed within 48 hours and again at 5 days.

8.3 Clinical failure is defined as no improvement in fever, respiratory rate, or oxygen saturation after 96 hours of treatment. Failure requires reassessment, repeat chest radiograph, and consideration of admission.

8.4 A follow-up chest radiograph at 8 weeks is recommended for patients aged 60 years and older, for current smokers, and for those with persistent symptoms.

8.5 Patients should be advised that cough and fatigue may persist for up to 6 weeks after treatment is completed.

## 9. Adverse Effects and Discontinuation

9.1 Common adverse effects (affecting 1 in 100 to 1 in 10 patients) include nausea, diarrhoea, and mild rash. These usually resolve without stopping treatment.

9.2 Uncommon adverse effects include elevated liver enzymes, vaginal or oral candidiasis, and headache.

9.3 Antibiox must be discontinued immediately in cases of angioedema, widespread rash, severe persistent diarrhoea with blood or fever, or jaundice.

9.4 The full prescribed course should be completed unless an event listed in 9.3 has occurred.

9.5 Antibiox courses must not be extended beyond 21 days under this guideline without specialist microbiology advice.

## 10. Special Populations

10.1 **Older adults.** Patients aged 65 years and older should be treated as at least moderate severity, and renal function should be measured before initiation.

10.2 **Patients with diabetes.** Apply 7.2 and monitor capillary glucose at least twice daily.

10.3 **Patients with hypertension.** Apply 7.1 and avoid prolonged withholding of Cardiolan beyond 72 hours after hydration is restored.

10.4 **Patients with chronic airway disease.** Apply 7.3 and review within 48 hours regardless of CRB score.

10.5 **Patients with kidney disease.** Apply the dose adjustments in Section 6 and the monitoring rule in 7.4.

## 11. Patient Counselling

11.1 Patients should be advised to rest, maintain oral fluid intake of at least 1.5 litres per day unless otherwise directed, and stop smoking.

11.2 Patients should be advised to seek urgent help for worsening breathlessness, confusion, blue lips, or inability to keep fluids down.

11.3 Patients should be told that most people feel better within 5 to 7 days, but that the full course must still be completed.

## 12. Illustrative Case Note

12.1 Patient A, aged 71, with hypertension treated with Cardiolan and type 2 diabetes treated with Glucorex, presents with fever, productive cough, and a respiratory rate of 24 breaths per minute. The eGFR is 44 mL/min/1.73 m². Because the patient is aged 65 years or older, 10.1 applies and moderate severity is assumed.

12.2 Under 4.2 and 6.3, the Antibiox dose of 500 mg is halved to 250 mg orally every 6 hours, giving 1000 mg per day, for 10 days. Under 7.2, Glucorex is withheld while oral intake is poor and restarted 72 hours after normal eating resumes, and under 7.1, Cardiolan is withheld until hydration is restored. Review is arranged within 48 hours under 8.2.
