# Asthma Guideline (Pulmavent)

## 1. Purpose and Scope

1.1 This guideline sets out the synthetic standard of care for the reliever treatment of asthma in adults using Pulmavent. It is a fictional document created for software testing and has no clinical validity.

1.2 It applies to adults aged 18 years and older with a confirmed diagnosis of asthma.

1.3 It does not apply to children, to chronic obstructive airway disease without an asthma diagnosis, or to long-term preventer therapy, which is outside the scope of this version.

1.4 Pulmavent is the sole bronchodilator addressed in this guideline. Other fictional agents are referenced only where they interact with Pulmavent or where a comorbidity changes management.

## 2. Definitions and Diagnosis

2.1 Asthma is diagnosed in a patient with episodic wheeze, breathlessness, chest tightness, or cough, together with objective evidence of variable airflow limitation.

2.2 Variable airflow limitation is confirmed by an improvement in forced expiratory volume in 1 second of 12% and 200 mL or more after Pulmavent, or by peak flow variability of 20% or more over 2 weeks of twice-daily recordings.

2.3 Peak expiratory flow (PEF) is expressed as a percentage of the patient's personal best or predicted value, whichever is higher.

2.4 An acute exacerbation is classed as mild to moderate when PEF is 50% to 75%, severe when PEF is 33% to 49%, and life-threatening when PEF is below 33%.

## 3. Control Assessment and Targets

3.1 Asthma is considered well controlled when reliever use is needed on no more than 2 days per week, night waking occurs no more than twice per month, and activity is not limited.

3.2 Asthma is considered poorly controlled when reliever use is needed on more than 2 days per week, or when night waking occurs weekly or more often.

3.3 Use of more than 8 puffs of Pulmavent in 24 hours is a danger sign and requires urgent clinical review on the same day.

3.4 The target is a PEF of 80% or more of personal best, with reliever use meeting the criteria in 3.1.

3.5 Control should be assessed at every review using the criteria in 3.1 and 3.2.

## 4. Dosing and Administration

4.1 Pulmavent is supplied as a metered-dose inhaler delivering 100 micrograms per puff.

4.2 For symptom relief, the recommended dose is 1 to 2 puffs (100 to 200 micrograms) as needed, with at least 4 hours between routine doses.

4.3 For exercise-induced symptoms, the dose is 2 puffs (200 micrograms) taken 15 minutes before exercise.

4.4 The maximum dose outside an exacerbation is 8 puffs (800 micrograms) in 24 hours.

4.5 For a mild to moderate exacerbation, the dose is 4 puffs via a spacer, repeated every 20 minutes for up to 1 hour, then reassessed. The maximum in this hour is 12 puffs.

4.6 For a severe exacerbation, the dose is 10 puffs via a spacer while emergency help is called. In hospital, nebulised Pulmavent 2.5 mg is given every 20 minutes for up to 3 doses.

4.7 The inhaler should be shaken before each use, and puffs should be given one at a time with 30 to 60 seconds between puffs. A spacer is recommended for all patients.

## 5. Contraindications and Cautions

5.1 Pulmavent is contraindicated in patients with a documented hypersensitivity to Pulmavent or any of its excipients.

5.2 Caution is advised in patients with cardiac arrhythmia, recent myocardial infarction, uncontrolled hyperthyroidism, or uncontrolled hypokalaemia.

5.3 Caution is advised in patients with a resting heart rate above 110 beats per minute, in whom Pulmavent doses should be kept to the lowest effective level.

5.4 Paradoxical bronchospasm after a dose is rare but requires immediate discontinuation and urgent assessment.

5.5 In pregnancy, Pulmavent may be used when the clinical benefit outweighs the risk.

## 6. Renal and Metabolic Considerations

6.1 No dose adjustment of Pulmavent is required for any level of renal function.

6.2 For patients with an eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73 m² who use more than 8 puffs in 24 hours on 2 or more consecutive days, serum potassium should be measured.

6.3 High doses of Pulmavent may lower serum potassium. A serum potassium below 3.5 mmol/L requires review of the dose and consideration of replacement.

6.4 Pulmavent may cause a small, transient rise in blood glucose, which is relevant for patients with diabetes as described in Section 7.

## 7. Drug Interactions

7.1 **Cardiolan.** Cardiolan may partially antagonise the effect of Pulmavent. In patients with asthma, Cardiolan should be used with caution, and Pulmavent response should be reviewed 2 weeks after Cardiolan is started. If wheeze worsens, Cardiolan should be stopped and an alternative class considered.

7.2 **Glucorex.** Patients taking Glucorex who use Pulmavent more than 4 times per day should have fasting glucose reviewed within 2 weeks.

7.3 **Thyrosan.** Excess thyroid hormone may increase heart rate and tremor when combined with Pulmavent. Heart rate should be reviewed if the resting rate exceeds 100 beats per minute, and Thyrosan dosing should be checked.

7.4 **Renaprotect.** In patients taking Renaprotect, potassium should be checked if Pulmavent use exceeds 8 puffs in 24 hours on 2 or more consecutive days.

7.5 **Antibiox.** No dose adjustment of Pulmavent is required during a course of Antibiox. If asthma worsens during a respiratory infection, pneumonia should be considered and assessed using GL-03.

7.6 **Neurazol.** No clinically significant interaction between Neurazol and Pulmavent is recognised.

7.7 **Zentavir.** No clinically significant interaction between Zentavir and Pulmavent is recognised.

## 8. Monitoring and Follow-Up

8.1 Patients with poorly controlled asthma should be reviewed every 4 weeks until control is achieved.

8.2 Once control is stable, review is required every 12 months, including assessment of inhaler technique and spacer use.

8.3 A 2-week peak flow diary, recorded morning and evening, should be completed before any treatment review.

8.4 Following an exacerbation, the patient should be reviewed within 48 hours and again at 2 weeks.

8.5 Adherence and technique should be assessed at every visit, and a written personal action plan should be provided.

## 9. Adverse Effects and Discontinuation

9.1 Common adverse effects (affecting 1 in 100 to 1 in 10 patients) include fine tremor, palpitations, and headache. These are dose-related and usually settle with continued use.

9.2 Uncommon adverse effects include muscle cramps, sleep disturbance, and nervousness.

9.3 Rare adverse effects include arrhythmia, hypokalaemia, and paradoxical bronchospasm.

9.4 Pulmavent must be discontinued immediately in cases of paradoxical bronchospasm, severe arrhythmia, or angioedema.

9.5 Pulmavent is an as-needed treatment and does not require tapering on discontinuation.

## 10. Special Populations

10.1 **Older adults.** Start with 1 puff (100 micrograms) as needed and monitor for tremor and palpitations.

10.2 **Patients with hypertension.** Apply 7.1 before any Cardiolan initiation and document the review at 2 weeks.

10.3 **Patients with diabetes.** Apply 7.2 and advise on glucose monitoring if reliever use rises.

10.4 **Patients with thyroid disease.** Apply 7.3 and review heart rate at each visit.

10.5 **Pregnant women.** Apply 5.5 and continue reliever treatment during exacerbations, as uncontrolled asthma carries greater risk than treatment.

## 11. Patient Counselling

11.1 Patients should be advised to carry their reliever inhaler at all times and to check the dose counter regularly.

11.2 Patients should be advised to seek emergency help if 10 puffs give no relief, if speech becomes difficult, or if lips turn blue.

11.3 Patients should be told that a rising need for Pulmavent is a sign of worsening control and should prompt review rather than self-escalation.

## 12. Illustrative Case Note
