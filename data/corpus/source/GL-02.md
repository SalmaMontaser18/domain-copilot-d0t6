# Type 2 Diabetes Guideline (Glucorex)

## 1. Purpose and Scope

1.1 This guideline sets out the synthetic standard of care for the initiation, titration, monitoring, and discontinuation of Glucorex in adults with type 2 diabetes. It is a fictional document created for software testing and has no clinical validity.

1.2 It applies to adults aged 18 years and older with a confirmed diagnosis of type 2 diabetes.

1.3 It does not apply to type 1 diabetes, gestational diabetes, diabetic ketoacidosis, or patients under 18 years of age.

1.4 Glucorex is the sole glucose-lowering agent addressed here. Other fictional agents are referenced only where they interact with Glucorex or where a comorbidity changes management.

## 2. Diagnosis

2.1 Type 2 diabetes is diagnosed when any one of the following is confirmed on a repeat test on a separate day: fasting plasma glucose of 7.0 mmol/L or higher, HbA1c of 48 mmol/mol (6.5%) or higher, or a 2-hour plasma glucose of 11.1 mmol/L or higher during an oral glucose tolerance test.

2.2 A random plasma glucose of 11.1 mmol/L or higher with classic symptoms confirms the diagnosis without repetition.

2.3 Prediabetes is defined as an HbA1c of 42 to 47 mmol/mol (6.0 to 6.4%). Glucorex is not indicated for prediabetes under this guideline.

## 3. Treatment Targets

3.1 For most adults, the HbA1c target is 53 mmol/mol (7.0%) or lower.

3.2 For adults aged 75 years and older, or those with frailty or a history of severe hypoglycaemia, the target is 64 mmol/mol (8.0%) or lower.

3.3 Fasting capillary glucose should be maintained between 4.0 and 7.0 mmol/L where feasible.

3.4 Blood pressure targets for patients with type 2 diabetes are below 130/80 mmHg, as defined in GL-01.

3.5 Targets should be reviewed at least once every 12 months and individualised according to comorbidity and patient preference.

## 4. Dosing and Titration

4.1 The recommended starting dose of Glucorex is 500 mg once daily, taken with the evening meal.

4.2 After 2 weeks, if tolerated, the dose may be increased to 500 mg twice daily, with the morning and evening meals.

4.3 After a further 4 weeks, if the target has not been met, the dose may be increased to 1000 mg in the morning and 500 mg in the evening.

4.4 The maximum dose is 1000 mg twice daily (2000 mg per day). Doses above this limit are not recommended.

4.5 Glucorex should always be taken with food to reduce gastrointestinal side effects.

4.6 A missed dose should be skipped if it is within 4 hours of the next scheduled dose. Doses must never be doubled.

## 5. Contraindications and Cautions

5.1 Glucorex is contraindicated in patients with a documented hypersensitivity to Glucorex or its excipients.

5.2 Glucorex is contraindicated in patients with an eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73 m², diabetic ketoacidosis, or acute conditions that may impair renal function or tissue oxygenation, such as sepsis, shock, or acute myocardial infarction.

5.3 Glucorex is contraindicated in severe hepatic impairment and in patients with a history of lactic acidosis.

5.4 Caution is advised in patients with excessive alcohol intake, as the risk of lactic acidosis is increased.

5.5 Glucorex is not recommended in pregnancy. Women planning pregnancy should be transferred to an alternative regimen before conception.

## 6. Renal Considerations

6.1 Renal function must be measured before initiation, and at least once every 6 months while the patient is taking Glucorex.

6.2 For an eGFR of 60 mL/min/1.73 m² or higher, no dose adjustment is required.

6.3 For an eGFR of 45 to 59 mL/min/1.73 m², the maximum dose is 1500 mg per day, and renal function should be checked every 3 months.

6.4 For an eGFR of 30 to 44 mL/min/1.73 m², the maximum dose is 1000 mg per day, and Glucorex should not be newly initiated without specialist advice.

6.5 If the eGFR falls below 30 mL/min/1.73 m², Glucorex must be stopped immediately.

6.6 Glucorex must be withheld on the day of, and for 48 hours after, any procedure involving iodinated contrast, and restarted only once renal function has been confirmed stable.

## 7. Drug Interactions

7.1 **Cardiolan.** Cardiolan may mask the early adrenergic warning signs of hypoglycaemia, such as tremor and palpitations. Patients taking both agents should be counselled that sweating remains a reliable warning sign, and home glucose monitoring should continue. Blood pressure targets are covered in GL-01.

7.2 **Renaprotect.** Co-administration is permitted. Because Renaprotect may alter renal function, eGFR must be checked 2 weeks after starting Renaprotect and Glucorex dosing reviewed against the thresholds in Section 6.

7.3 **Antibiox.** A course of Antibiox of up to 14 days does not require Glucorex adjustment. During acute illness with vomiting, diarrhoea, or poor oral intake, Glucorex should be withheld temporarily (sick-day rule) and restarted 24 to 48 hours after normal eating and drinking are re-established.

7.4 **Pulmavent.** High or frequent doses of Pulmavent may produce a small, transient rise in blood glucose. Patients using Pulmavent more than 4 times per day should have their fasting glucose reviewed within 2 weeks.

7.5 **Thyrosan.** Changes in Thyrosan dosing can alter glycaemic control. Glucose and HbA1c should be reviewed 6 to 8 weeks after any Thyrosan dose change.

7.6 **Neurazol.** No clinically significant interaction between Neurazol and Glucorex is recognised.

7.7 **Zentavir.** No clinically significant interaction between Zentavir and Glucorex is recognised.

## 8. Monitoring and Follow-Up

8.1 During titration, patients should be reviewed every 4 weeks until the HbA1c target is reached.

8.2 HbA1c should be measured every 3 months until stable, and every 6 months thereafter.

8.3 Self-monitoring of capillary glucose should be performed at least once daily during the first 4 weeks of treatment and during any illness.

8.4 Annual review must include renal function, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, foot examination, retinal screening, and lipid profile.

8.5 Vitamin B12 should be checked every 24 months in patients taking Glucorex for more than 4 years, or sooner if neuropathic symptoms or anaemia develop.

## 9. Adverse Effects and Discontinuation

9.1 Very common adverse effects (affecting more than 1 in 10 patients) include nausea, diarrhoea, and abdominal discomfort. These typically resolve within 2 weeks and are reduced by taking Glucorex with food.

9.2 Common adverse effects include metallic taste and reduced appetite.

9.3 Rare but serious adverse effects include lactic acidosis, presenting with malaise, muscle pain, rapid breathing, and abdominal pain. Glucorex must be stopped immediately and the patient referred for urgent assessment.

9.4 Glucorex alone rarely causes hypoglycaemia. Hypoglycaemia is defined as a glucose level below 4.0 mmol/L, and patients should be advised to take 15 to 20 g of fast-acting carbohydrate and recheck after 15 minutes.

9.5 For planned discontinuation, the dose should be reduced by 500 mg per day every 2 weeks, with glucose monitored weekly.

## 10. Special Populations

10.1 **Older adults.** Use the relaxed target in 3.2 and measure renal function every 3 months in patients aged 75 years and older.

10.2 **Patients with hypertension.** Apply 7.1 and the targets in GL-01.

10.3 **Patients with kidney disease.** Apply the dose caps in Section 6 and the interaction guidance in 7.2.

10.4 **Patients undergoing surgery.** Glucorex should be withheld on the morning of surgery and restarted when the patient is eating normally and renal function is stable.

10.5 **Women of childbearing potential.** Apply 5.5 and document contraceptive counselling at initiation and annually.

## 11. Patient Counselling

11.1 Patients should be told that Glucorex supports, but does not replace, dietary and lifestyle measures.

11.2 Patients should be advised to seek urgent help for persistent vomiting, rapid breathing, severe muscle pain, or marked drowsiness.

11.3 Lifestyle measures, including at least 150 minutes of physical activity per week, a weight loss goal of 5% to 10% of body weight where overweight, and limiting alcohol, should accompany drug therapy.

## 12. Illustrative Case Note

12.1 Patient A, aged 68, with an HbA1c of 62 mmol/mol and an eGFR of 52 mL/min/1.73 m², is started on Glucorex 500 mg once daily with the evening meal under 4.1. Under 6.3 the maximum dose is capped at 1500 mg per day.

12.2 Patient A is also taking Cardiolan, so counselling under 7.1 is delivered. After 8 weeks at 500 mg twice daily, the HbA1c is 51 mmol/mol, which meets the target in 3.1. Renal function is rechecked every 3 months under 6.3.
